# Expense ratio and total expense ratio (TER)

The **expense ratio** is the annual fee a mutual fund charges to run the scheme,
expressed as a percentage of assets. It covers fund management, administration,
registrar fees, and — in a regular plan — distributor commission. The regulator
calls the all-in number the **total expense ratio**, or TER.

The fee is not billed to you separately. It is deducted from the fund's assets
daily, so the NAV you see is already net of expenses. You never notice it leaving,
which is exactly why it is worth checking.

## Typical ranges in India

- Index funds and ETFs: 0.05% to 0.50%
- Actively managed large-cap equity: 0.8% to 1.8% (regular), 0.4% to 1.0% (direct)
- Debt funds: 0.2% to 1.2%
- SEBI caps equity TER on a sliding scale that falls as the scheme's assets grow.

## Why a small number matters

Fees compound against you the same way returns compound for you. Two funds that
earn the same 11% gross return but charge 0.4% versus 1.6% will, over 25 years,
differ by roughly a third of the final corpus. Active management can justify a
higher fee only if it reliably beats a low-cost index after costs — and most do
not over long periods.

## What to do

Before buying, look up the scheme's current TER (it is on the AMC site and the
factsheet). Prefer the direct plan. For core equity exposure, a broad index fund
with a sub-0.3% TER is hard to beat on cost. Reserve higher-fee active funds for
areas where you have a specific reason to expect an edge.
