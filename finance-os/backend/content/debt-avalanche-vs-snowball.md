# Paying down debt: avalanche vs snowball

When you carry several debts at once, the order you clear them in changes how
much interest you pay and how motivated you stay. Two methods are common.

## Avalanche — cheapest

Pay the minimum on every debt, then put all spare money against the debt with the
**highest interest rate**. When it is gone, roll its payment into the next-highest
rate. Mathematically optimal: it minimises total interest and total time to
debt-free. A 42% credit-card balance costs you far more per month than a 9% home
loan of the same size, so it goes first.

## Snowball — most motivating

Same minimums, but you attack the **smallest balance** first regardless of rate.
You clear individual debts quickly, which frees up a full EMI slot and gives a
visible win. It usually costs a little more interest than avalanche, but people
are more likely to finish the plan.

## Which to use

- If your highest-rate debt is also small, the two methods agree — start there.
- If you have been discouraged by slow progress, snowball's early wins are worth
  the small extra cost.
- Otherwise avalanche, because high-interest consumer debt (credit cards,
  personal loans, BNPL) compounds fast enough that speed matters.

## The rule that comes first

Before either method: stop adding new high-interest debt. Clear the statement
balance on cards every cycle. High-interest debt sits above investing in the
priority order — a guaranteed 40% saved beats an uncertain 12% earned.
