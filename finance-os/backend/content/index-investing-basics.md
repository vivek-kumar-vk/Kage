# Index investing basics

An **index fund** buys every stock in a market index in the same proportion as
the index — for example the Nifty 50 or a total-market index. It does not try to
pick winners. It simply owns the market and charges very little to do so.

## Why it works

- **Cost.** A broad index fund charges 0.05% to 0.30% a year. The average active
  fund charges several times that, and the fee is a certain drag on return.
- **Odds.** Over 10-plus years, most active funds underperform their benchmark
  after costs. The few that win are hard to identify in advance.
- **Behaviour.** There is no manager to second-guess and no style that falls out
  of favour, so it is easier to hold through a downturn.

## How to use it

1. Decide an asset split between equity and debt that matches your time horizon
   and how much loss you can sit through.
2. Fill the equity side with one or two broad index funds. Add a global index
   fund for diversification if you want exposure outside India.
3. Invest a fixed amount every month regardless of the market level.
4. Rebalance once a year back to your target split.
5. Leave it alone otherwise.

## What it is not

Index investing does not remove market risk — a broad index can still fall 30% to
50% in a bad year. It removes the *added* risk of picking the wrong manager or
the wrong stock, and it removes a large slice of cost. Over decades that is
usually enough.
