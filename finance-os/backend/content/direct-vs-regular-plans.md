# Direct vs Regular mutual fund plans

Every mutual fund scheme in India comes in two variants: **direct** and **regular**.
They hold the identical portfolio, run by the same fund manager. The only
difference is distribution cost.

- A **regular** plan pays a trail commission to the distributor or platform that
  sold it. That commission is baked into the scheme's expense ratio, so it is
  deducted from your returns every year for as long as you hold the units.
- A **direct** plan has no distributor, so its expense ratio is lower — typically
  0.5% to 1.0% lower per year for actively managed equity funds, less for index
  funds and debt funds.

That yearly gap compounds. On a 20-year horizon a 0.75% drag can reduce the final
corpus by roughly 12 to 15 percent. Nothing about the fund's strategy changed;
you simply stopped paying for distribution you were not using.

## How to tell which one you hold

Look at the scheme name on your statement. A direct plan says "Direct" or
"Direct Plan" in the name. Anything without that word is a regular plan. The ISIN
also differs between the two variants of the same scheme.

## Switching

You can move from regular to direct, but a switch is treated as a redemption plus
a fresh purchase. That can trigger capital-gains tax and, for equity funds held
under a year, an exit load. The usual approach is to stop fresh investments into
the regular plan, start new SIPs in the direct plan, and redeem the old units
gradually in a tax-aware way.

## The takeaway

If you choose your own funds and do not rely on a distributor for advice, the
direct plan is almost always the better choice. The saving is small each year and
large over a lifetime.
