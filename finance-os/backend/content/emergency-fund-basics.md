# Building an emergency fund

An **emergency fund** is cash set aside to cover essential expenses when income
stops or an unplanned bill lands — a job loss, a medical event, an urgent repair.
Its job is to keep a temporary problem from becoming a debt spiral.

## How much

The common guideline is **three to six months of essential monthly expenses** —
rent or EMI, food, utilities, insurance premiums, school fees, transport. Count
only what you could not skip for a few months, not your full lifestyle spend.

- Stable salaried income, two earners: three to four months is usually enough.
- Single income, variable pay, or a sector prone to layoffs: aim for six months
  or more.
- Add a buffer if you support dependents or carry a home loan.

## Where to keep it

The fund must be safe and reachable within a day or two, not invested for growth.
Reasonable places:

- A sweep-in fixed deposit linked to your savings account.
- A liquid or overnight mutual fund (understand the exit timing and the small
  market risk).
- Plain savings account for the first month's worth, the rest slightly less
  liquid for a marginally better yield.

Do not keep it in equity, in a long-duration debt fund, or locked in a
multi-year FD. The point is certainty, not return.

## Building it

If starting from zero, treat the fund as a fixed monthly transfer that comes out
before any discretionary spending, until it is full. Once it is funded, leave it
alone and redirect that cash flow to investing. Refill it promptly after any use.
